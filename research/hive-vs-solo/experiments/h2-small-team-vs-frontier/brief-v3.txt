## Difficulty

These problems are for a benchmark where earlier problems rated 2200-3000
were solved by every model tested, including small fast models. Write
problems that strong AI models get WRONG. Target Codeforces 2800-3500
(Div. 1 E/F level), each needing at least two separate non-obvious
insights, where:

- the natural first idea, and the second idea a strong solver tries after
  the first fails, are both wrong or too slow (put the most tempting one in
  naive.py);
- the statement examples are small and do NOT reveal the key insight (a
  wrong idea should still pass all the examples);
- correctness depends on edge cases that random small tests do catch (so
  brute.py comparison is meaningful), not on hidden wording tricks.

The problem must still be hard because of the algorithm, never because of
vague wording: a strong contestant who reads it once must know exactly what
to compute. Avoid well-known textbook problems in disguise.

## Files per problem (all required)

- statement.md   Markdown: title as "# Title", story-free or short story,
                 precise rules, "## Input", "## Output", "## Constraints",
                 and "## Examples" with 2-4 examples (Input/Output in ``` blocks).
                 Answers must be unique (or use a float checker, see meta).
- examples.json  [{"input": "...", "output": "..."}] exactly matching the
                 statement's examples (inputs end with a newline).
- gen.py         def generate(rng: random.Random, size: str) -> str
                 size is "small", "medium" or "large". Returns one full stdin
                 text. "small" must be tiny enough for brute.py (< 0.5 s each)
                 yet varied enough to hit edge cases (include n=1, equal
                 values, extremes, adversarial shapes with some probability).
                 "large" must hit the maximum constraints and the worst case
                 for slow approaches. Use only rng for randomness.
- brute.py       def solve(inp: str) -> str  Exhaustive/obviously-correct
                 solution for small inputs. Written independently of ref.py,
                 by the most direct definition of the answer.
- ref.py         def solve(inp: str) -> str  The intended efficient solution in
                 pure CPython (standard library only). On every "large" input
                 it must finish in at most 3 seconds on a laptop; aim for 1 s.
                 Choose constraints so that this is true for the intended
                 algorithm in Python (e.g. n <= 2*10^5 for O(n log n)).
- naive.py       def solve(inp: str) -> str  The tempting wrong or too-slow
                 approach. It MUST fail: either wrong on some small input, or
                 more than 4x slower than ref on large inputs.
- meta.json      {"title": "...", "topic": ["tag", ...], "author": "codex:gpt-6.1-sol",
                  "checker": "tokens"}   (or "float:1e-6" for real outputs)

solve() returns the exact expected stdout text (e.g. "42\n" or lines joined).

## Validation (mandatory)

After writing a problem, run:

    python3 {GRADER} validate <problem_dir>

It prints JSON. "ok": true is required. It checks: ref matches every
example, ref == brute on 300 small inputs, small outputs are not
near-constant (at least 3 distinct), ref finishes large inputs in under 10 s,
and naive.py fails. Fix and re-run until ok. Also personally check that
ref_large_secs is at most 3. If you cannot make a problem pass after honest
effort, delete its directory and write a different one.
