The two instrument defects, named

1. Reference error. Golden references that encode one valid answer and silently fail equivalents. Found twice by the same cheap procedure: read failed deliveries against the written criteria, blind to the reference's specific answer. Both fixes added equivalence language; the pass/fail contract never changed.

2. Measurement-pipeline error. MAX_OUTPUT_CHARS=4000 truncated deliveries before the grader saw them — differentially punishing the verbose tier and producing E3's odd "terra scores lower while both fail" inversion. Now aligned to the audit window (6,000). Rule: store what you grade; any cap upstream of the grader is part of the instrument.

Why this is the paper

Apparent market failure in a deployed agent marketplace decomposes into (i) true capability gaps, (ii) reference error, (iii) pipeline error — and (ii)+(iii) were the majority here. Simulation testbeds cannot observe those two terms: their references and pipes are correct by construction. Only a deployed system with a reference-graded audit lane can measure them, and ours just did, at class-flipping magnitude, twice in one day. "Audit the auditor" is not hygiene; it is where most of the correction came from.
